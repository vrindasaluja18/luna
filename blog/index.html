Liquid memory

What a CPU-only SLAM stack is actually for — and the one quantity that can govern its clustering, its eviction, and its reward.

vrinda ·

Two principles

Nothing is true unless it is in maths

Prose is for intuition. If a claim can't be written as an equation or measured as a number, it isn't a claim yet — it's a feeling. The practical form, for a codebase: every quantity needs a consumer that changes a decision; every buffer needs a justification in bytes; every README claim needs a measurement behind it.

Compute is precious

Every line must answer: what decision does this change? If the answer is nothing, it's a cost with no benefit. The first, most primitive version of the question is the one I couldn't answer for a long time — which thing would be on DRAM?

The pipeline, and where the bytes live

Hover or tap a stage to see what it holds. Bar width is proportional to peak bytes resident. The estimator and the pose graph are O(1) and O(keyframes); only the map fields scale with area — and the largest transient is not the SLAM.

The ratio that matters isn't bytes — it's bytes held per decision served. 43 MB of flood produces one corridor graph the planner queries once per plan. Driving that ratio down is the goal.

The finding

The topology module is already persistence-based clustering, and doesn't know it.

include/luna/topology/morse.hpp
Scalar min_persistence = 0.20;   // declared, documented at length,
                                 // never read. One occurrence in the repo:
                                 // its own declaration.

Scalar min_bottleneck_contrast = 0.75;  // what actually decides
                                        // "is this a room": a density
                                        // contrast test.

So the room/doorway decision is made by asking “does the vehicle have to squeeze through?” — which is exactly the criterion of Chazal, Guibas, Oudot & Skraba, Persistence-Based Clustering in Riemannian Manifolds, J. ACM 2013:

A cluster is a region of high density, separated from other such regions by low density. Combine a mode-seeking phase with a merging phase; merge two clusters when the density between them is high relative to their own.

That is a finding, not a bug to patch. The correct maths was already in the system; the only reason it looked like a heuristic is that nobody had written down what it was a special case of.

Three flows — only one is liquid

flowformin Luna?
A · supervised ẋ = fθ(x,t),   dL/dθ = ∫ λᵀ ∂f/∂θ dt no — no autograd, no tape, no ODE solver
B · occupancy ℓ ← clamp(ℓ + Δ, −4, +4) yes — saturated, already implemented
C · topological fidelity ∝ persistence target

Flow B is a flow with a bounded state, which makes the map a contraction: recent evidence wins, old evidence decays, one bad measurement can't flip a long-free cell. What it lacks is a term for memory — −λ·(fidelity pressure). That missing λ is the first real work.

Liquid memory, defined as a flow rather than a slogan: hold each region at a fidelity proportional to its persistence, and release it when persistence falls below what its DRAM cost can justify.

Who does what, when there is no RL

There is no reinforcement learning in Luna. The roles are filled by classical components — and the missing rows are the whole story.

They are missing for one reason: the route follower is scripted, so the sensor stream does not depend on any decision. There is nothing to optimise a policy against. That is not an oversight — it is the boundary of the design, and why “Luna learns” can't be claimed yet.

The plan

  1. Phase 0Inventory and delete dead compute. Name the consumer of every buffer above 1 MB. No new maths.start here
  2. Phase 1The reward as a pure function of a recorded run: scoreRun → {PE, HE, BD, task}. No learning.next
  3. Phase 2A store of seeded runs; the reward ranks routes across them.research
  4. Phase 3A bandit over route choices. “Learning” first becomes true here.research
  5. Phase 4Active SLAM — open the action space. Only if Phase 3 shows signal.later

The first two phases are the blog. The last two are the research.
